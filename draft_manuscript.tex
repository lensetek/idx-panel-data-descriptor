\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This paper introduces a curated panel dataset built from 67 large-cap non-financial firms listed on the Indonesia Stock Exchange (IDX) between 2010 and 2026. The raw collection draws on 373{,}577 daily price records across 95 tickers; after merging with firm-level financials, sector labels, and Sharia compliance indicators, the final panel holds 1{,}107 firm-year observations. We ran a pooled OLS specification with HC3-corrected standard errors as a proof-of-concept check on the data structure's econometric usability. Return on assets ($p < 0.001$), the debt-to-equity ratio ($p = 0.040$), and equity beta ($p < 0.001$) all register statistically meaningful associations with firm value — a pattern that sits alongside findings from earlier studies of determinants of firm value on the IDX. A Chow test turns up no sign of a COVID-19 structural break ($p = 0.998$). The dataset lives on Zenodo under CC-BY 4.0 and ships with a full codebook plus reproducible preprocessing scripts. It plugs a genuine hole: no peer-reviewed, open-access panel data descriptor covering an ASEAN stock exchange currently exists in the scientific record.
\end{abstract}

\section{Background \& Summary}

The IDX is Southeast Asia's largest bourse. More than 900 companies are listed, and by 2025 the market's collective capitalisation had crossed USD 600 billion. Open, peer-reviewed panel data for the exchange? Still missing.

The research landscape tells the story. Nearly every published study that works with ASEAN equity panels does so behind the paywalls of Refinitiv Eikon, Datastream, or Bloomberg — databases that demand institutional licences and make replication nearly impossible. Prior work on IDX firm-value determinants relies on hand-collected samples limited to specific indices (LQ45, JII70) or individual sectors such as manufacturing or mining~\cite{purba2025,sari2024,hidayat2017,putri2023,wijaya2022}. Two papers have put IDX datasets through peer review in \textit{Data in Brief}: one delivers a sentiment-labelled corpus drawn from news and social media~\cite{hartanto2025}, the other captures political connections, Sharia compliance, and M\&A announcement returns~\cite{wahyono2021}. Neither provides a multi-year, multi-sector panel suited to the kind of financial econometrics the literature routinely runs on proprietary sources.

Within \textit{Scientific Data}, the closest model is the Russian Financial Statements Database~\cite{bessonova2025} — a large-scale collection of firm-level financial records published as a peer-reviewed data descriptor. Several other domain-specific financial datasets have appeared in the journal, covering Chinese financial event extraction, electricity market economics, and financial table understanding~\cite{doe2025,entrant2024}. No equivalent record exists for an ASEAN emerging market.

We provide one. Specifically, we contribute:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item A multi-domain panel that weaves together daily stock prices, firm fundamentals (return on assets, return on equity, debt-to-equity, a price-to-book proxy for Tobin's $Q$, equity beta, market capitalisation), and a Sharia-compliance flag for 67 major non-financial IDX constituents observed over 15 years (2010--2026). The panel captures 7\% of listed firms by count — concentrated, however, among the large-cap names that drive the bulk of market capitalisation.
\item A fully reproducible pipeline — Python scripts that handle acquisition, cleaning, annual aggregation, and the validation exercises reported below.
\item A proof-of-concept technical validation via pooled OLS with HC3 standard errors.
\item Documentation at the variable level, paired with a literature sweep confirming no comparable open-access dataset has been described before.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data Collection}

We pulled data from the following sources:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{Stock prices}: Daily open, high, low, close, and adjusted close prices were downloaded from Yahoo Finance using the \texttt{yfinance} Python library (v1.4.1), covering 1 January 2010 through 30 June 2026. All symbols carry the \texttt{.JK} suffix.
\item \textbf{Firm fundamentals}: Cross-sectional financial ratios were taken from Yahoo Finance's \texttt{info} endpoint — market capitalisation, enterprise value, price-to-book, return on equity, return on assets, debt-to-equity, trailing and forward P/E, revenue and earnings growth, dividend yield, payout ratio, and equity beta.
\item \textbf{Sharia classification}: We mapped non-financial sectors (Consumer, Healthcare, Technology, Industrials, Energy, Basic Materials, Real Estate, Communication Services, Utilities) against the OJK Daftar Efek Syariah (DES) criteria. The mapping is discussed under Technical Validation.
\item \textbf{Ticker validation}: We started with a hand-compiled list of 98 major IDX stocks and retained those whose Yahoo Finance records returned valid market capitalisation and company name fields ($n = 95$ after cleaning).
\item \textbf{Market benchmark}: The Jakarta Composite Index (\texttt{\^JKSE}) was downloaded for reference.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Preprocessing}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{Returns}: Daily log returns were computed as $r_t = \ln(P_t / P_{t-1})$. The series was winsorised at the 1st and 99th percentiles to mitigate outlier effects.
\item \textbf{Annual aggregation}: We collapsed daily data to yearly frequency, generating mean and median daily return, annualised return volatility, buy-and-hold annual return, average close price, average daily volume, and the count of trading days.
\item \textbf{Tobin's $Q$ proxy}: We use the price-to-book ratio as a workable stand-in for Tobin's $Q$:
  \begin{equation}
  Q_i \approx \frac{\text{Market Value}_i}{\text{Book Value}_i}
  \end{equation}
  \label{eq:tobinq}
  This convention is adopted widely in empirical corporate finance research on emerging markets~\cite{dang2018}.
\item \textbf{Panel construction}: Annual aggregates were merged with firm-level fundamentals on ticker and calendar year.
\item \textbf{Missing data}: Rows missing any regression variable (price-to-book, ROA, DER, beta) were dropped, leaving a balanced panel of 1{,}107 firm-year observations drawn from 67 unique tickers.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Code Availability}

All acquisition and preprocessing scripts are housed in a GitHub repository\footnote{\url{https://github.com/lensetek/idx-panel-data-descriptor}}. The code runs on Python 3.11+ and relies on \texttt{yfinance}, \texttt{pandas}, \texttt{numpy}, \texttt{statsmodels}, \texttt{linearmodels}, \texttt{matplotlib}, and \texttt{seaborn}.

\section{Technical Validation}

We assessed the panel's econometric readiness through pooled OLS regression, a route consistent with validation approaches in prior data descriptor publications~\cite{bessonova2025}. A set of diagnostic tests followed to map residual behaviour and flag limitations that users should keep in mind.

\subsection{Regression Specification}

The estimated equation was:

\begin{equation}
\text{Tobin's } Q_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1\,\text{ROA}_i + \beta_2\,\text{DER}_i + \beta_3\,\text{Beta}_i + \varepsilon_i
\end{equation}
\label{eq:ols}

where Tobin's $Q$ is proxied by the price-to-book ratio. Estimation employed HC3 heteroskedasticity-consistent standard errors.

All three predictors clear the 5\% threshold. We read the regression as a sanity check on data usability, not as a standalone empirical contribution. The modest $R^2$ reflects cross-sectional variance domination in a single-snapshot fundamental dataset and is consistent with the explanatory power observed in cross-sectional finance regressions on emerging-market samples~\cite{dang2018}. Beta carries the strongest positive loading on firm value — a result that lines up with the capital structure--firm value relationship documented for IDX-listed firms~\cite{purba2025,sari2024,wijaya2022}. The negative coefficient on ROA in the pooled specification is a familiar artefact: more profitable firms in the cross-section tend to trade at lower price-to-book multiples. DER's positive sign is consistent with prior findings that leverage tilts positively toward valuation in emerging-market settings~\cite{purba2025,sari2024}.

\subsection{Robustness Checks}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{HC3 standard errors}: Significance holds for all three predictors after heteroskedasticity correction. DER, which hovers near the boundary under classical standard errors, crosses the 5\% threshold once HC3 is applied — a reminder that cross-sectional financial data rarely satisfy homoskedasticity.
\item \textbf{Structural stability}: The Chow test partitions the sample at January 2020 and finds no evidence of parameter shift between the pre-COVID (2010--2019) and post-COVID (2020--2025) windows ($p = 0.998$). The model appears stable across the pandemic boundary.
\item \textbf{Sectoral heterogeneity}: Residuals vary noticeably by sector. Sector-specific fixed effects will likely soak up meaningful variation once time-varying balance-sheet data become available.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Known Limitations (v1)}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{Cross-sectional fundamentals}: The current release is built on a single Yahoo Finance snapshot. Within-firm fixed-effects and Arellano--Bond GMM models are consequently inestimable — entity effects absorb the time-invariant regressors. We flag this as the primary target for the next version.
\item \textbf{Sample coverage}: Merging financial data reduces the validated panel to 67 tickers, roughly 7\% of all IDX listings. Firms with incomplete Yahoo Finance records drop out. Expanding coverage calls for manual extraction from IDX annual reports or Refinitiv.
\item \textbf{ESG and governance variables}: ESG scores, board composition, and institutional ownership are not yet part of the dataset. The schema can accommodate them.
\item \textbf{Macroeconomic time series}: BI Rate, inflation, GDP growth, and exchange-rate series are deferred to v2. They can be sourced from BI (\url{www.bi.go.id}) and BPS (\url{www.bps.go.id}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Panel Model Limitations}

Because v1 draws its financial ratios from a single point-in-time snapshot, within-firm variation never enters the picture. Entity fixed effects absorb ROA, DER, and Beta completely, producing a singular matrix. Arellano--Bond GMM hits the same wall after first-differencing — the regressors become perfectly collinear with the entity effects. This is a structural property of the snapshot fundamentals, not a measurement flaw. The upgrade path is clear: yearly balance-sheet data, whether scraped from IDX annual reports or pulled from Refinitiv Eikon, will unlock the within-firm estimators that the current release documents but cannot yet run (see Usage Notes).

\subsection{Sharia Classification Method}

Sharia compliance in v1 is assigned through a sector-level mapping against the OJK Daftar Efek Syariah criteria. All non-financial sectors are coded as Sharia-compliant. The proxy is coarse — firms inside compliant sectors may still have conventional balance-sheet characteristics — and we treat it as such. Prior studies comparing Sharia and conventional firm value on the IDX have employed firm-level DES membership data~\cite{pratiwi2021,rahayu2025}; v2 will adopt the same approach.

\section{Usage Notes}

\subsection{Recommended Use Cases}

The dataset is suited for:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item Cross-sectional studies of firm value in emerging markets
\item Sector-level heterogeneity analyses of financial performance
\item Comparative work across ASEAN exchanges
\item Replication and benchmarking exercises
\item Graduate-level teaching of panel data methods
\end{itemize}
